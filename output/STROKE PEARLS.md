# STROKE PEARLS & COMMON MISTAKES

## Learning Objective

To reinforce high-yield concepts in stroke recognition, diagnosis, imaging, acute management, and prevention through concise clinical pearls and awareness of common diagnostic and management errors.

---

# Stroke Pearls (High-Yield Clinical Points)

- **Time is Brain**
- Every minute of untreated ischemic stroke results in the loss of approximately **1.9 million neurons**.
- Early recognition and rapid treatment significantly improve functional outcomes.

---

# 2. Always Differentiate Ischemic Stroke from Haemorrhagic Stroke

- Clinical examination alone **cannot reliably distinguish** between the two.
- **Non-Contrast CT Brain** is the first-line imaging investigation.

---

# 3. Never Forget Blood Glucose

- Hypoglycemia is the **most common reversible stroke mimic**.
- Always check capillary blood glucose before initiating stroke-specific treatment.

---

# 4. Normal CT Does NOT Exclude Early Ischemic Stroke

- CT may appear normal during the first few hours.
- MRI with Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI) is more sensitive for early ischemic changes.

---

# 5. Posterior Circulation Stroke is Frequently Missed

Symptoms may include:

- Vertigo
- Ataxia
- Diplopia
- Dysarthria
- Dysphagia
- Sudden imbalance

---

# 6. Every Suspected Stroke Requires NIHSS Assessment

The **National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS)** provides a standardized neurological assessment and helps determine stroke severity.

---

# 7. Large Vessel Occlusion Requires Rapid Recognition

Suspect large vessel occlusion when patients present with:

- Dense hemiplegia
- Aphasia
- Forced gaze deviation
- Neglect
- High NIHSS score
These patients may benefit from **mechanical thrombectomy**.

---

# 8. Blood Pressure Management is Stroke-Type Specific

- Elevated blood pressure may be protective in acute ischemic stroke.
- Aggressive blood pressure reduction may worsen cerebral perfusion.
- Haemorrhagic stroke requires careful blood pressure control to reduce hematoma expansion.

---

# 9. Dysphagia Screening Saves Lives

Every stroke patient should undergo swallowing assessment before oral intake to reduce the risk of aspiration pneumonia.

---

# 10. Secondary Prevention Begins Immediately

Long-term management should focus on:

- Blood pressure control
- Lipid management
- Diabetes control
- Smoking cessation
- Physical activity
- Appropriate antithrombotic therapy

---

# Common Mistakes in Stroke Management

---

# Mistake 1

- Starting thrombolysis **before excluding intracranial haemorrhage**.
- Correct Practice:
Always perform a **Non-Contrast CT Brain** first.

---

# Mistake 2

- Ignoring sudden dizziness because there is no limb weakness.
- Correct Practice:
Consider **posterior circulation stroke**, especially when vertigo is associated with neurological signs.

---

# Mistake 3

- Missing hypoglycemia.
- Correct Practice:
Check **blood glucose immediately** in every suspected stroke patient.

---

# Mistake 4

- Assuming a normal CT excludes stroke.
- Correct Practice:
Early ischemic stroke may not be visible on CT. Consider MRI if clinically indicated.

---

# Mistake 5

- Delaying activation of the stroke team.
- Correct Practice:
Early stroke pathway activation reduces door-to-needle and door-to-puncture times.

---

# Mistake 6

- Giving food or water before swallowing assessment.
- Correct Practice:
Perform dysphagia screening first.

---

# Mistake 7

- Ignoring atrial fibrillation on ECG.
- Correct Practice:
Always investigate for a cardioembolic source in ischemic stroke.

---

# Mistake 8

- Focusing only on acute treatment.
- Correct Practice:
Secondary prevention is equally important to reduce recurrent stroke risk.

# FAST vs BE-FAST

| **FAST** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| **F** | Facial drooping |
| **A** | Arm weakness |
| **S** | Speech difficulty |
| **T** | Time to call emergency services |

| **BE-FAST** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| **B** | Balance difficulty |
| **E** | Eye/visual disturbance |
| **F** | Facial weakness |
| **A** | Arm weakness |
| **S** | Speech difficulty |
| **T** | Time to seek immediate medical care |

**Clinical Pearl:** BE-FAST improves recognition of **posterior circulation strokes**, which may be missed using FAST alone.

---

# NIHSS Quick Interpretation

| **NIHSS Score** | **Stroke Severity** |
| --- | --- |
| **0** | No neurological deficit |
| **1–4** | Minor stroke |
| **5–15** | Moderate stroke |
| **16–20** | Moderate to severe stroke |
| **21–42** | Severe stroke |

# Imaging Pearls

| **Finding** | **Clinical Significance** |
| --- | --- |
| Hyperdense MCA Sign | Early middle cerebral artery thrombosis |
| Loss of Gray-White Differentiation | Early ischemic infarction |
| Sulcal Effacement | Cerebral oedema |
| Hyperdense Intraparenchymal Area | Intracerebral haemorrhage |
| DWI Bright + ADC Dark | Acute ischemic stroke |
| FLAIR Positive | Established infarction (later stage) |

# Stroke Mnemonics

### FAST

- **F** – Face drooping
- **A** – Arm weakness
- **S** – Speech difficulty
- **T** – Time is Brain

### BE-FAST

- **B** – Balance
- **E** – Eyes
- **F** – Face
- **A** – Arm
- **S** – Speech
- **T** – Time

### ABCDE During Initial Assessment

- **A** – Airway
- **B** – Breathing
- **C** – Circulation
- **D** – Disability (Neurological Examination)
- **E** – Exposure

### Stroke Risk Factors – "SHADES"

- **S** – Smoking
- **H** – Hypertension
- **A** – Atrial fibrillation
- **D** – Diabetes mellitus
- **E** – Elevated cholesterol
- **S** – Sedentary lifestyle
