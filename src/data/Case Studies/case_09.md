# Key Learning Points

1. Posterior circulation strokes commonly present with vertigo, vomiting, ataxia, dysphagia, and hoarseness rather than limb weakness.
2. A normal non-contrast CT scan does not exclude cerebellar infarction.
3. **MRI-DWI** is the gold standard for early diagnosis of posterior fossa ischemic stroke.
4. CT angiography is essential to identify vertebral artery or PICA occlusion.
5. Crossed sensory loss with ipsilateral Horner syndrome is characteristic of **Lateral Medullary (Wallenberg) Syndrome**.
6. Posterior circulation strokes may have low NIHSS scores despite causing severe disability, so clinical judgment remains crucial.

# Possible Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized presentation:

- **Gelsemium sempervirens** – when dizziness, weakness, heaviness, and sluggishness predominate in the individualized symptom picture.
- **Conium maculatum** – if vertigo aggravated by turning the head matches the patient's totality.
- **Causticum** – during rehabilitation for persistent neurological weakness when constitutionally indicated.
- **Arnica montana** – if the patient's overall symptom profile corresponds to vascular trauma and soreness.
- **Cocculus indicus** – when vertigo and nausea predominate and the remedy picture fits.

### Reason for Selection

Remedies are prescribed according to individualized classical homoeopathic principles and not solely because of the diagnosis of PICA infarction or Wallenberg syndrome.

# Clinical Pearls

1. Acute vertigo with hoarseness, dysphagia, and ataxia should always raise suspicion for a **posterior circulation stroke**—not just a peripheral vestibular disorder.
2. **Lateral Medullary (Wallenberg) Syndrome** is most commonly caused by PICA or vertebral artery occlusion.
3. A normal CT scan does not exclude cerebellar infarction; **MRI-DWI** is much more sensitive in the posterior fossa.
4. Crossed sensory loss (**ipsilateral facial and contralateral body pain/temperature deficits**) is a classic feature of lateral medullary syndrome.
5. Early swallow assessment is essential because aspiration pneumonia is a major complication.
6. Patients with disabling posterior circulation deficits may benefit from **intravenous thrombolysis** when they meet eligibility criteria.
7. Vestibular and balance rehabilitation play a major role in functional recovery after cerebellar stroke.
