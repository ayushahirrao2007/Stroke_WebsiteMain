# Why is Age Important?

The incidence of haemorrhagic stroke increases significantly after 55 years of age due to:

- Progressive degeneration of cerebral blood vessels.
- Long-standing hypertension.
- Diabetes mellitus.
- Age-related vascular fragility.

Older patients are also at increased risk of:

- Hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage.
- Cerebral amyloid angiopathy.
- Poor neurological recovery.
- Increased mortality due to raised intracranial pressure and secondary brain injury.

Therefore, advanced age is an important predictor of both stroke occurrence and clinical outcome.

The basal ganglia receive blood supply from the **lenticulostriate arteries**, which are small penetrating branches of the **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA)**.

These vessels are particularly susceptible to rupture in patients with long-standing hypertension because of **Charcot–Bouchard microaneurysm formation**.

# HOMOEOPATHIC PERSPECTIVE (AFTER STABILIZATION)

# Illustrative Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized symptom picture, a homoeopathic physician may consider:

- **Arnica montana** – when the totality of symptoms corresponds to trauma-like soreness and vascular injury.
- **Lachesis mutus** – if the individualized symptom profile matches its characteristic features.
- **Opium** – in selected cases with profound stupor and sluggish responsiveness, based on the totality of symptoms.
- **Baryta carbonica** – in elderly patients with an appropriate constitutional picture.
- **Causticum** – when characteristic paralysis-related features predominate during rehabilitation.

### Reason for Selection

Remedy selection is based on the **individualized symptom totality and classical homoeopathic principles**, not solely on the diagnosis of haemorrhagic stroke.
