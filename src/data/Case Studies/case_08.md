# Key Learning Points

1. Primary intraventricular HAEMORRHAGE is an uncommon but life-threatening subtype of HAEMORRHAGIC stroke.
2. Sudden deterioration in consciousness with headache and vomiting should immediately raise suspicion for IVH with hydrocephalus.
3. Non-contrast CT is the investigation of choice for rapid diagnosis.
4. The **Graeb Score** helps quantify intraventricular blood volume and assess severity.
5. CTA is essential to exclude aneurysms and vascular malformations.
6. Acute obstructive hydrocephalus often requires urgent neurosurgical intervention with an **External Ventricular Drain (EVD)**.

# Possible Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized presentation:

- **Arnica montana** – when the overall symptom picture suggests vascular trauma and soreness.
- **Opium** – for profound stupor or reduced responsiveness if the remedy picture matches.
- **Lachesis mutus** – in selected patients whose symptom profile corresponds to the remedy characteristics.
- **Baryta carbonica** – in elderly hypertensive patients when constitutionally indicated.
- **Causticum** – during rehabilitation for persistent neurological weakness if supported by the totality of symptoms.

### Reason for Selection

Remedies are prescribed based on individualized homoeopathic principles and not solely because of the diagnosis of intraventricular HAEMORRHAGE.

# Clinical Pearls

1. **Primary intraventricular HAEMORRHAGE** is a neurosurgical emergency because blood obstructs cerebrospinal fluid flow, leading to acute hydrocephalus.
2. Rapid deterioration in consciousness, projectile vomiting, and **Cushing's response** are classic indicators of raised intracranial pressure.
3. **Non-contrast CT** is the quickest and most effective investigation for diagnosing IVH and hydrocephalus.
4. The **Graeb Score** helps estimate the severity of intraventricular blood burden and prognosis.
5. **External Ventricular Drain (EVD)** placement is the definitive treatment for obstructive hydrocephalus due to IVH.
6. Early airway protection, blood pressure control, and neurocritical care improve survival and neurological outcomes.
7. Early multidisciplinary rehabilitation is essential for maximizing functional recovery after stabilization.
