---

# Key Learning Points

1. Lobar HAEMORRHAGE in elderly patients should immediately raise suspicion for **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy**.
2. **NCCT** is the first-line investigation for acute HAEMORRHAGIC stroke.
3. **CTA** is essential to exclude aneurysms and arteriovenous malformations.
4. **MRI SWI/GRE** is the investigation of choice for detecting cerebral microbleeds associated with CAA.
5. The **Boston Criteria** help establish the diagnosis of probable Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy without the need for brain biopsy.

# Possible Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized presentation:

- **Arnica montana** – when vascular trauma and soreness predominate in the remedy picture.
- **Lachesis mutus** – if the patient's overall symptom profile corresponds to the remedy characteristics.
- **Opium** – in selected cases with marked stupor or reduced responsiveness matching the remedy picture.
- **Baryta carbonica** – in elderly patients with constitutional indications.
- **Causticum** – during rehabilitation for persistent motor weakness when indicated by the totality of symptoms.

### Reason for Selection

Remedies are selected according to classical individualized homoeopathic principles and not solely on the diagnosis of cerebral amyloid angiopathy or intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE.

# Follow-Up Advice

- Regular Neurology follow-up.
- Strict blood pressure monitoring.
- Continue physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and cognitive rehabilitation.
- Avoid unnecessary anticoagulants or antiplatelets unless specifically advised.
- Seek immediate medical attention if new neurological deficits, severe headache, vomiting, seizures, or reduced consciousness develop.

# Clinical Pearls

1. Lobar HAEMORRHAGE in patients older than 70 years should strongly suggest **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA)**.
2. **Non-contrast CT** is the first-line imaging modality for diagnosing intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE.
3. **MRI SWI/GRE sequences** are highly sensitive for detecting cortical microbleeds typical of CAA.
4. The **Boston Criteria** help establish the diagnosis of probable CAA without brain biopsy.
5. Routine thrombolysis, anticoagulation, and unnecessary antiplatelet therapy are contraindicated during the acute HAEMORRHAGIC event.
6. Blood pressure control, careful neurological monitoring, and rehabilitation remain the cornerstones of management.
7. Patients with CAA have an increased risk of recurrent lobar HAEMORRHAGE and require long-term follow-up.
