# Why CT Brain First?

Acute blood appears bright (**hyperdense**) on non-contrast CT, allowing rapid diagnosis of HAEMORRHAGIC stroke and guiding emergency management.

---

# CT Interpretation

## Hyperdense Lesion

Represents fresh intracerebral blood within the left putamen.

## Vasogenic OEDEMA

Develops around the hematoma due to disruption of the blood–brain barrier, increasing intracranial pressure.

## Midline Shift

Indicates mass effect from the expanding hematoma and raises concern for neurological deterioration.

# Why CTA?

CTA helps exclude structural causes of HAEMORRHAGE such as aneurysms or AVMs, especially in atypical presentations.

## Target Blood Pressure

The reduction should be controlled rather than abrupt.

### Why was Nicardipine/Labetalol used?

- Reduces the risk of hematoma expansion.
- Lowers blood pressure in a controlled manner.
- Maintains cerebral perfusion while limiting further bleeding.

### Why not lower BP rapidly?

Excessive reduction can reduce cerebral perfusion around the hematoma and worsen neurological injury.

# Possible Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized presentation:

- **Arnica montana** – when the overall symptom picture suggests vascular trauma and soreness.
- **Lachesis mutus** – selected in cases with symptom characteristics corresponding to the remedy profile, including left-sided complaints.
- **Opium** – in selected patients with profound stupor or reduced responsiveness matching the remedy picture.
- **Baryta carbonica** – considered in elderly hypertensive individuals when constitutionally indicated.
- **Causticum** – during rehabilitation for persistent motor weakness if supported by the totality of symptoms.

### Reason for Selection

Remedies are chosen according to individualized homoeopathic principles and not solely because the diagnosis is intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE.

# Clinical Pearls

1. Severe headache, vomiting, and reduced consciousness strongly favor **HAEMORRHAGIC stroke** over ischemic stroke.
2. Non-contrast CT is the first-line investigation because acute blood appears hyperdense.
3. The basal ganglia are the most common site of hypertensive intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE due to rupture of lenticulostriate arteries.
4. Careful blood pressure reduction helps prevent hematoma expansion without compromising cerebral perfusion.
5. Routine thrombolysis and anticoagulation are contraindicated in acute intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE.
6. Raised intracranial pressure should be recognized and managed promptly.
7. Early multidisciplinary rehabilitation improves functional recovery and quality of life.
8. Long-term blood pressure control is the most effective strategy to reduce the risk of recurrent hypertensive intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE.
