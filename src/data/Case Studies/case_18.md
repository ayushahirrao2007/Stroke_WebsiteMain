## 8. WHY IS AGE IMPORTANT?

The incidence of **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)** increases after **60 years** because of progressive **atherosclerosis** affecting the carotid and cerebral arteries.

Advancing age contributes to:

- Carotid artery plaque formation

- Reduced vascular elasticity

- Endothelial dysfunction

- Increased prevalence of hypertension and dyslipidaemia

- Higher risk of thromboembolic events

### Clinical Correlation

The **ophthalmic artery** is the first major branch of the **internal carotid artery**. Temporary embolic occlusion of this artery causes **Amaurosis Fugax**, producing sudden painless monocular vision loss that resolves completely once retinal perfusion is restored.

This event is considered a **retinal TIA** and should be managed with the same urgency as a cerebral TIA because it is a warning sign of impending ischemic stroke.
