## 8. WHY IS AGE IMPORTANT?

Lacunar strokes are most frequently seen in **older adults**, especially after the age of **60 years**, due to progressive damage to the small penetrating arteries supplying deep brain structures.

Advancing age contributes to:

- Lipohyalinosis of small penetrating arteries

- Long-standing hypertension

- Diabetes-related microangiopathy

- Endothelial dysfunction

- Increased risk of small-vessel cerebrovascular disease

### Clinical Correlation

The **ventral posterolateral (VPL) nucleus of the thalamus** receives sensory information from the opposite side of the body. A small infarct in this region produces **pure sensory stroke**, characterized by contralateral numbness affecting the face, arm, trunk, and leg without weakness or cortical dysfunction.

### Clinical Correlation

The **Ventral Posterolateral (VPL) nucleus** of the thalamus receives sensory information from the **contralateral body**, while the **Ventral Posteromedial (VPM) nucleus** receives sensory input from the **contralateral face**.

A small infarction affecting these sensory relay nuclei results in **Pure Sensory Lacunar Stroke**, characterized by:

- Contralateral hemisensory loss

- No motor weakness

- No aphasia

- Monitor blood pressure and blood glucose at home.

- Follow a low-salt, diabetic-friendly diet.

- Engage in regular physical activity after medical clearance.

- Attend Neurology and Diabetology follow-up appointments.

- Seek emergency medical care immediately if any of the following occur:

  - Sudden weakness

  - New numbness

  - Slurred speech

  - Facial drooping

  - Vision changes

  - Loss of balance
