# Learning Points

- Right MCA strokes commonly produce left hemiparesis, left hemispatial neglect, left homonymous hemianopia, and right gaze preference.
- Unlike left MCA strokes, aphasia is usually absent because the dominant language cortex is typically spared.
- Hemispatial neglect is a hallmark feature of non-dominant hemisphere involvement.
- CTA is essential for identifying large vessel occlusion and guiding acute treatment decisions.
- Early recognition and rapid imaging are critical to maximize the chance of favorable outcome.

# Homoeopathic Perspective (After Medical Stabilization)

Homoeopathic management is considered only after emergency stroke care is complete and the patient is medically stable. It serves as a complementary approach during rehabilitation and does not replace evidence-based acute stroke treatment.

## Comprehensive Homoeopathic Case-Taking

- Constitutional characteristics
- Mental generals
- Physical generals
- Individual symptom totality
- Modalities
- Past and family history
- Miasmatic assessment

## Illustrative Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized symptom picture, a homoeopathic physician may consider:

- **Causticum** – when paralysis-related symptoms correspond to the remedy picture.
- **Gelsemium** – if profound weakness and heaviness predominate.
- **Baryta carbonica** – in selected elderly individuals with an appropriate constitutional profile.
- **Lachesis** – if the patient's overall symptom picture matches the remedy characteristics.
- **Arnica montana** – in selected cases where the totality of symptoms supports its use.

**Reason for selection:** Remedy choice is based on individualized homoeopathic principles and the complete symptom picture, not on the diagnosis of stroke alone.

# Clinical Pearls

1. Right MCA strokes often present without aphasia, making neglect an important diagnostic clue.
2. Left hemispatial neglect is a hallmark of non-dominant parietal lobe involvement.
3. Patients with anosognosia may deny or be unaware of their deficits despite obvious paralysis.
4. Right gaze preference is a common sign of acute right MCA infarction.
5. Non-contrast CT is the first-line imaging modality to exclude intracranial haemorrhage.
6. CT angiography identifies large vessel occlusion and assists in treatment planning.
7. Early rehabilitation is essential for improving functional recovery and reducing disability.
8. Occupational therapy is particularly important for patients with hemispatial neglect.
9. Long-term control of hypertension, dyslipidaemia, and other vascular risk factors reduces the likelihood of recurrent stroke.
10. Complementary homoeopathic management, when used, should always follow stabilization and should never delay or replace evidence-based emergency stroke care.
