## 7. WHY IS AGE IMPORTANT?

The risk of **intracerebral haemorrhage** increases after the age of **60 years** because of progressive degeneration of small penetrating cerebral arteries.

Long-standing hypertension causes:

- Lipohyalinosis

- Charcot–Bouchard microaneurysm formation

- Fragility of deep perforating arteries

- Increased susceptibility to spontaneous rupture

The **basal ganglia** is one of the most common sites of hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage.

## 36. REHABILITATION

### Physiotherapy

- Passive and active range-of-motion exercises

- Muscle strengthening

- Gait training

- Balance training

### Occupational Therapy

- Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)

- Hand function rehabilitation

- Fine motor coordination

- Home safety education

### Speech Therapy

- Dysarthria management

- Communication exercises

- Swallow rehabilitation (if required)

**Psychological Support**

- Emotional counselling

- Family education

- Anxiety and depression screening

## 37. SECONDARY PREVENTION

The patient and family receive counselling regarding:

- Strict lifelong blood pressure control

- Regular medication adherence

- Blood glucose optimization

- Lipid management

- Low-salt, heart-healthy diet

- Weight reduction

- Regular exercise after medical clearance

- Smoking cessation and avoidance of excessive alcohol

- Routine Neurology follow-up

- Recognition of stroke warning signs using **BE-FAST**

- Immediate emergency evaluation if new neurological symptoms develop

## Doctor's Thought Process

> *"The strongest predictor of recurrent hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage is poorly controlled blood pressure. Long-term adherence to treatment and regular follow-up are essential to prevent recurrence."*

## 38. DISCHARGE SUMMARY

## Final Diagnosis

**Acute Hypertensive Intracerebral Haemorrhage**

**Location:** **Right Basal Ganglia**

**Etiology:** Rupture of **lenticulostriate arteries** secondary to long-standing uncontrolled hypertension.
