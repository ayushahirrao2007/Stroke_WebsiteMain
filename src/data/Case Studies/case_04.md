# Learning Points

1. PCA strokes often present without limb weakness, making diagnosis more challenging.
2. Right homonymous hemianopia is the most common neurological deficit in dominant PCA infarction.
3. Alexia without agraphia is a classical sign of left PCA infarction involving the splenium of the corpus callosum.

5. MRI is particularly valuable for detecting early posterior circulation infarcts that may be subtle on CT.

# Homoeopathic Perspective (After Medical Stabilization)

Homoeopathic treatment may be considered only after emergency medical management is complete and the patient is clinically stable. It should complement, not replace, evidence-based stroke care.

## Individualized Homoeopathic Assessment

- Mental generals
- Physical generals
- Constitutional characteristics
- Family history
- Miasmatic background
- Complete symptom totality

## Possible Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized case:

- **Natrum muriaticum** – constitutional cases with visual disturbances and emotional factors.
- **Gelsemium sempervirens** – heaviness, weakness, and sluggish neurological recovery.
- **Causticum** – persistent neurological deficits and motor dysfunction when indicated.
- **Aurum metallicum** – emotional changes or depressive features after stroke.
- **Anacardium orientale** – selected cognitive disturbances based on symptom similarity.

**Reason for Selection:** Remedies are chosen according to the totality of symptoms and constitutional assessment, not solely on the diagnosis of stroke.

# Clinical Pearls

1. PCA strokes often present without limb weakness, making them easy to miss.
2. Right homonymous hemianopia is the hallmark visual field defect of a left PCA infarction.
3. Alexia without agraphia is a classic feature of dominant PCA infarction involving the splenium of the corpus callosum.
4. A low NIHSS score does not necessarily indicate a minor stroke—visual and cognitive deficits can be profoundly disabling.
5. MRI is more sensitive than CT for detecting early posterior circulation infarcts.
6. Visual rehabilitation and occupational therapy are essential components of recovery.
7. Secondary prevention through risk factor modification significantly reduces recurrent stroke risk.
8. Homoeopathic management, if used, should be individualized and integrated only after stabilization, never as a substitute for acute stroke treatment.
