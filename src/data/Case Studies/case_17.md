## 8. WHY IS AGE IMPORTANT?

The incidence of posterior circulation ischemic stroke increases significantly after **60 years** because of progressive atherosclerosis and small-vessel disease.

Advancing age contributes to:

- Atherosclerosis

- Endothelial dysfunction

- Reduced vascular elasticity

- Increased prevalence of hypertension and diabetes

- Higher risk of vertebrobasilar circulation disease

### Clinical Correlation

The **Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (PICA)** supplies the inferior cerebellar hemisphere and parts of the medulla. Infarction in this territory commonly presents with:

- Vertigo

- Vomiting

- Nystagmus

- Ipsilateral limb ataxia

- Gait imbalance

- Occipital headache

Unlike anterior circulation strokes, significant limb weakness may be absent.

### Illustrative Remedy Considerations

Depending upon the individualized symptom totality:

- **Conium maculatum** – where vertigo is aggravated by turning the head or changing position.

- **Cocculus indicus** – when vertigo, nausea, vomiting, and imbalance predominate.

- **Gelsemium sempervirens** – when dizziness is associated with weakness, heaviness, and incoordination.

- **Bryonia alba** – when symptoms worsen with movement and improve with rest.

- **Arnica montana** – in selected cases during the recovery phase when indicated by the constitutional picture.

**Note:** Remedy selection should always be individualized and based on the totality of symptoms, not solely on the diagnosis.

- Regular Neurology follow-up

## Doctor's Thought Process

> *"Posterior circulation strokes have a significant risk of recurrence if vascular risk factors remain uncontrolled. Long-term prevention is as important as acute treatment."*

## 38. DISCHARGE SUMMARY

## Final Diagnosis

**Acute Right Cerebellar Ischemic Stroke**

**Stroke Subtype:** **Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (PICA) Territory Infarction**

**Etiology:** Acute thrombotic occlusion of the **Right Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery**, most likely secondary to vertebrobasilar atherosclerotic disease.
