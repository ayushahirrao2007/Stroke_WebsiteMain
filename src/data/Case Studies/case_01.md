- Pupils equal and reactive
- Extraocular movements normal

**Why?**

# Medication Education Box

> These are examples of medications that may be considered in evidence-based stroke care depending on the patient's eligibility and current clinical guidelines. They are shown here for educational purposes to explain the clinical reasoning.

## 1. Intravenous Thrombolytic Therapy (When Eligible)

### Purpose

To dissolve the clot and restore blood flow to the affected part of the brain.

### Why was it considered?

- Symptom onset was recent.
- Brain imaging excluded haemorrhage.
- Significant neurological deficit was present.

### Why might it not be given?

Examples include:

- Intracranial haemorrhage
- Certain recent major surgeries
- High bleeding risk
- Other contraindications identified during assessment

### Expected Benefit

Early reperfusion may improve neurological recovery in eligible patients.

### Doctor's Thought Process

> "The patient arrived early. Because imaging excluded haemorrhage, we must determine whether he meets the eligibility criteria for reperfusion therapy."

## 2. Antiplatelet Therapy

### Why is it considered?

To reduce the risk of recurrent ischemic stroke in appropriate patients.

### Why not give it immediately to everyone?

The timing depends on the patient's treatment pathway and confirmation that intracranial haemorrhage has been excluded.

## 3. High-Intensity Statin Therapy

### Why?

- Stabilizes atherosclerotic plaque.
- Reduces cholesterol.
- Lowers future stroke risk.

### Expected Benefit

Secondary prevention of future vascular events.

## 4. Blood Pressure Management

### Why was blood pressure monitored?

The patient's BP was elevated.

Excessively high blood pressure may increase complications, while excessive reduction can reduce blood flow to the ischemic penumbra.

### Doctor's Thought Process

> "Blood pressure management in acute stroke is individualized. We avoid unnecessary rapid reductions because the brain may depend on higher pressures to maintain perfusion."

## 5. Blood Glucose Management

### Why?

Both hypoglycaemia and marked hyperglycaemia can worsen neurological outcomes.

## 6. Intravenous Fluids

### Why were IV fluids considered?

Patients with acute stroke may have reduced oral intake, vomiting, or dehydration. Maintaining adequate hydration supports circulation and cerebral perfusion while avoiding fluid overload.

## 7. Oxygen Therapy

### Was oxygen started?

**No.**

### Why?

The patient's oxygen saturation remained normal on room air. Supplemental oxygen is generally reserved for patients with hypoxaemia.

# Homoeopathic Perspective (After Stabilization)

Homoeopathic care should begin only after emergency stroke management has been completed and the patient is medically stable. It is intended as a complementary approach during rehabilitation and must not replace evidence-based acute stroke treatment.

## Homoeopathic Case-Taking

- Constitution
- Mental generals
- Physical generals
- Characteristic symptoms
- Modalities
- Past history
- Family history
- Miasmatic assessment

## Illustrative Remedy Considerations

Depending on the individualized symptom picture, a homoeopathic physician may consider remedies such as:

- **Causticum** – when the overall symptom picture includes characteristic paralysis-related features.
- **Gelsemium** – if weakness and tremulousness match the remedy picture.
- **Baryta carbonica** – in selected elderly patients with an appropriate constitutional picture.
- **Lachesis** – when the individualized symptom profile corresponds to the remedy.
- **Arnica montana** – in selected situations where the totality of symptoms supports its use.

**Reason for selection:** Remedy choice is based on the patient's complete symptom totality and classical homoeopathic principles, not solely on the diagnosis of stroke.
