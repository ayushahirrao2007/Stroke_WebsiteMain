# Investigations in Stroke

## Learning Objective

To understand the appropriate investigations performed in suspected stroke, their clinical indications, diagnostic significance, and role in guiding acute management and secondary prevention.

---

# Why are Investigations Important?

Acute stroke is a time-sensitive neurological emergency. Investigations are performed to:

- Confirm the diagnosis of stroke.
- Differentiate ischemic stroke from intracerebral **HAEMORRHAGE**.
- Identify the site and cause of vascular occlusion.
- Detect stroke mimics and associated medical conditions.
- Determine eligibility for reperfusion therapies.
- Guide acute treatment and long-term secondary prevention.

# Neuroimaging Investigations

| Investigation | Why is it Performed? | What Does it Detect? | How Does it Influence Management? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-Contrast CT Brain (NCCT)** | First-line emergency imaging | Intracerebral **HAEMORRHAGE**, large infarcts, early ischemic signs | Determines whether thrombolysis is contraindicated |
| **MRI Brain (DWI, ADC, FLAIR)** | More sensitive for ischemia | Early infarction, posterior circulation stroke, lacunar infarcts | Confirms ischemic stroke when CT is normal |
| **CT Angiography (CTA)** | Evaluates cerebral arteries | Large vessel occlusion, arterial stenosis | Determines eligibility for mechanical thrombectomy |
| **MR Angiography (MRA)** | Alternative vascular imaging | Intracranial and extracranial vessel abnormalities | Assesses vascular anatomy without iodinated contrast |
| **CT Perfusion (CTP)** | Evaluates salvageable brain tissue | Ischemic core and penumbra | Helps select patients for extended-window thrombectomy |
| **Carotid Duplex Ultrasonography** | Assesses carotid circulation | Carotid stenosis or plaque | Identifies candidates for carotid endarterectomy or stenting |

---

# Cardiac Investigations

| Investigation | Purpose | Clinical Importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Electrocardiogram (ECG)** | Detects atrial fibrillation, myocardial infarction, arrhythmias | Identifies cardioembolic stroke source |
| **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE)** | Evaluates cardiac chambers and valves | Detects ventricular thrombus, valvular disease |
| **Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE)** | Better visualization of atria and aorta | Detects patent foramen ovale, atrial thrombi, aortic plaques |
| **Holter Monitoring (24–72 hours)** | Continuous rhythm monitoring | Detects paroxysmal atrial fibrillation not seen on routine ECG |

---

# Laboratory Investigations

| Investigation | Why is it Ordered? | Clinical Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Random Blood Glucose** | Excludes hypoglycemia as a stroke mimic | Immediate management decision |
| **Complete Blood Count (CBC)** | Detects anemia, infection, thrombocytopenia | Assesses baseline hematological status |
| **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT)** | Evaluates bleeding risk | Essential before thrombolysis |
| **Renal Function Tests (RFT)** | Assesses renal function | Determines safety of contrast administration |
| **Liver Function Tests (LFT)** | Detects hepatic dysfunction | Medication planning |
| **Serum Electrolytes** | Identifies metabolic abnormalities | Detects stroke mimics and complications |
| **HbA1c** | Assesses chronic glycemic control | Guides long-term diabetes management |
| **Lipid Profile** | Evaluates dyslipidemia | Determines need for statin therapy |
| **Cardiac Troponin** | Detects myocardial injury | Identifies concurrent acute coronary syndrome |

---

# Additional Investigations (Selected Patients)

| Investigation | When is it Recommended? |
|---|---|
| **D-Dimer** | Suspected cerebral venous sinus thrombosis or hypercoagulable state |
| **Autoimmune Profile (ANA, ANCA, antiphospholipid antibodies)** | Young stroke patients or suspected vasculitis |
| **Thrombophilia Screen** | Young patients with recurrent or unexplained stroke |
| **Toxicology Screen** | Suspected drug-related stroke |
| **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Analysis** | Suspected meningitis, encephalitis, or subarachnoid **HAEMORRHAGE** with negative CT |

---

# CT vs MRI Brain

| CT Brain | MRI Brain |
|---|---|
| Widely available | Higher sensitivity for ischemia |
| Rapid acquisition | Longer acquisition time |
| Excellent for detecting acute **HAEMORRHAGE** | Excellent for early ischemic stroke |
| Preferred first-line emergency imaging | Preferred when CT is inconclusive or posterior circulation stroke is suspected |
| Lower cost | Higher cost |

# Which Investigation Should Be Performed First?

## Emergency Stroke Evaluation

1. **Capillary Blood Glucose**
2. **Non-Contrast CT Brain**
3. **CT Angiography** (if large vessel occlusion is suspected)
4. **MRI Brain** (selected patients)
5. **ECG**
6. **Routine Blood Investigations**
7. **Echocardiography**
8. **Carotid Doppler**

---

# Investigation Decision Box

A patient presents with sudden right-sided weakness and aphasia.

### Step 1: Check Blood Glucose

- **Low** → Treat hypoglycemia.
- **Normal** → Continue stroke pathway.

### Step 2: Perform Non-Contrast CT Brain

- **HAEMORRHAGE present** → Manage as intracerebral **HAEMORRHAGE**.
- **No HAEMORRHAGE** → Suspect ischemic stroke.

### Step 3: Perform CT Angiography

- **Large vessel occlusion** → Consider mechanical thrombectomy.
- **No large vessel occlusion** → Continue medical management.

### Step 4: Cardiac Evaluation

- ECG ± Echocardiography
- Look for atrial fibrillation or cardiac embolic source.

### Step 5: Laboratory Tests

- CBC
- PT/INR
- Electrolytes
- HbA1c
- Lipid Profile

These findings guide acute treatment and secondary prevention.
