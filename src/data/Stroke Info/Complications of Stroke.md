# Complications of Stroke

## Learning Objective

To recognize the common neurological and systemic complications of stroke, understand their underlying mechanisms, identify early warning signs, and apply appropriate preventive and management strategies to improve patient outcomes.

---

# Why Do Stroke Complications Occur?

Stroke causes damage to brain tissue, resulting in neurological deficits, prolonged immobility, swallowing dysfunction, autonomic disturbances, and systemic physiological changes. These factors increase the risk of complications that may worsen neurological recovery or become life-threatening if not identified and managed promptly.

# 1. Cerebral Oedema

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Swelling of brain tissue following ischemic or haemorrhagic stroke due to fluid accumulation. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Breakdown of the blood-brain barrier and cellular injury lead to increased intracranial fluid. |
| **Clinical Features** | Severe headache, vomiting, decreasing consciousness, unequal pupils, worsening neurological deficits. |
| **Prevention** | Early stroke management, close neurological monitoring, control of blood pressure and oxygenation. |
| **Management** | Head elevation, osmotic therapy (mannitol or hypertonic saline), neurosurgical decompression in selected patients. |

---

# 2. Raised Intracranial Pressure (ICP)

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Increased pressure within the cranial cavity due to brain swelling or haemorrhage. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Cerebral oedema, hematoma expansion, or obstructive hydrocephalus. |
| **Clinical Features** | Altered consciousness, headache, vomiting, papilloedema, Cushing's triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respiration). |
| **Prevention** | Early detection and monitoring of neurological deterioration. |
| **Management** | Osmotic therapy, airway support, neurosurgical consultation when indicated. |

---

# 3. Haemorrhagic Transformation

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Bleeding into an area of ischemic infarction. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Reperfusion of damaged cerebral vessels after ischemia. |
| **Clinical Features** | Sudden neurological worsening, severe headache, reduced consciousness. |
| **Prevention** | Appropriate patient selection for thrombolysis and careful blood pressure control. |
| **Management** | Immediate neuroimaging, discontinuation of anticoagulants if appropriate, supportive care, neurosurgical evaluation when necessary. |

---

# 4. Aspiration Pneumonia

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Lung infection caused by aspiration of food, saliva, or gastric contents. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Dysphagia and impaired protective airway reflexes after stroke. |
| **Clinical Features** | Fever, productive cough, breathlessness, hypoxia, abnormal chest examination. |
| **Prevention** | Swallow assessment before oral feeding, aspiration precautions, speech and swallowing therapy. |
| **Management** | Appropriate antibiotics, oxygen therapy, hydration, respiratory support when required. |

---

# 5. Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Formation of thrombus in deep veins, usually of the lower limb. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Prolonged immobility and venous stasis after stroke. |
| **Clinical Features** | Leg swelling, calf tenderness, warmth, unilateral oedema. |
| **Prevention** | Early mobilization, intermittent pneumatic compression, pharmacological prophylaxis in selected patients. |
| **Management** | Anticoagulation according to current clinical guidelines. |

---

# 6. Pulmonary Embolism

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Obstruction of pulmonary arteries by emboli originating from deep veins. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Migration of thrombus from lower limb DVT. |
| **Clinical Features** | Sudden dyspnoea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, hypoxia. |
| **Prevention** | Prevention and early treatment of DVT. |
| **Management** | Oxygen therapy, anticoagulation, advanced management according to severity. |

---

# 7. Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Infection of the urinary tract during hospitalization. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Urinary catheterization, urinary retention, immobility. |
| **Clinical Features** | Fever, dysuria, urinary frequency, suprapubic discomfort. |
| **Prevention** | Avoid unnecessary catheterization, maintain catheter hygiene, early catheter removal. |
| **Management** | Urine culture-guided antibiotic therapy and adequate hydration. |

---

# 8. Pressure Injuries (Pressure Ulcers)

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Localized tissue damage caused by prolonged pressure over bony prominences. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Immobility and poor nutrition. |
| **Clinical Features** | Skin redness, ulceration, pain, tissue breakdown. |
| **Prevention** | Frequent repositioning, pressure-relieving mattresses, skin care, adequate nutrition. |
| **Management** | Wound care, infection control, pressure relief, nutritional optimization. |

---

# 9. Post-Stroke Seizures

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Seizures occurring after acute brain injury due to stroke. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Cortical irritation from ischemia or haemorrhage. |
| **Clinical Features** | Focal or generalized seizures, altered consciousness. |
| **Prevention** | No routine prophylactic Antiepileptics medication in patients without seizures. |
| **Management** | Antiepileptics medications in patients with documented seizures. |

---

# 10. Depression & Cognitive Impairment

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Emotional and cognitive disorders developing after stroke. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Brain injury, disability, psychosocial stress. |
| **Clinical Features** | Low mood, memory impairment, poor concentration, social withdrawal. |
| **Prevention** | Early psychological assessment, rehabilitation, family support. |
| **Management** | Counseling, cognitive rehabilitation, antidepressants when clinically indicated. |

---

# 11. Spasticity & Shoulder Pain

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **What is it?** | Increased muscle tone and painful limitation of shoulder movement. |
| **Why does it occur?** | Upper motor neuron injury and prolonged immobility. |
| **Clinical Features** | Muscle stiffness, painful shoulder, restricted movement. |
| **Prevention** | Early physiotherapy, proper limb positioning, range-of-motion exercises. |
| **Management** | Rehabilitation, muscle relaxants in selected patients, pain management. |

---

# Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Medical Attention

- Sudden deterioration in consciousness
- Recurrent seizures
- Persistent vomiting
- New neurological deficits
- Severe headache
- Chest pain or acute breathlessness
- High-grade fever with altered mental status
