# CT & MRI INTERPRETATION GUIDE IN STROKE

## Learning Objective

To develop a systematic approach for interpreting CT and MRI findings in patients with suspected stroke, enabling early recognition of ischemic stroke, intracranial HAEMORRHAGE, vascular occlusion, and other stroke-related abnormalities.

## Why is Imaging Important?

Brain imaging is the cornerstone of acute stroke diagnosis. It helps to:

- Confirm the diagnosis of stroke.
- Differentiate ischemic stroke from HAEMORRHAGIC stroke.
- Identify the affected vascular territory.
- Detect large vessel occlusion.
- Guide thrombolysis and thrombectomy decisions.
- Exclude stroke mimics such as TUMOURs or subdural hematoma.

## Systematic Approach to Brain Imaging

```text
           Patient with Suspected Stroke
                         │
                         ▼
               Non-Contrast CT Brain
                         │
        ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
        ▼                                 ▼
   HAEMORRHAGE                      No HAEMORRHAGE
        │                                 │
   ICH Protocol             Evaluate Early Ischemic Signs
                                          │
                                          ▼
                               CTA / MRI if indicated
                                          │
                                          ▼
                            Determine Vascular Territory
                                          │
                                          ▼
                                  Treatment Decision
```

# PART A — CT BRAIN INTERPRETATION

## Before Looking for Stroke…

Always review the CT systematically using the **ABCS approach**.

| **Step** | **What to Look For** |
| --- | --- |
| **A – Asymmetry** | Compare both cerebral hemispheres for density differences. |
| **B – Blood** | Look for hyperdense HAEMORRHAGE or intraventricular blood. |
| **C – Cerebrospinal Fluid Spaces** | Assess ventricles, basal cisterns, and sulci for compression or enlargement. |
| **S – Skull & Soft Tissues** | Look for fractures, scalp swelling, or extracranial abnormalities. |

---

# 1. Normal CT Brain

### Recognition Checklist

- Symmetrical cerebral hemispheres
- Preserved gray-white matter differentiation
- Normal ventricles
- No HAEMORRHAGE
- No midline shift

### Clinical Significance

A normal CT does **not exclude** acute ischemic stroke during the first few hours after symptom onset.

### NeuroNexus Pearl

![Normal CT Brain — Preserved Gray-White Differentiation](/images/ct_mri/ct_01_normal_brain.png)

A normal CT Brain should never delay further stroke evaluation when clinical suspicion remains high.

---

# 2. Hyperdense MCA Sign

![Hyperdense MCA Sign — Early Acute Thrombus](/images/ct_mri/ct_02_hyperdense_mca_sign.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Bright middle cerebral artery
- Increased arterial density
- Usually unilateral
- Appears before infarction develops

### Clinical Significance

- Represents **acute thrombus within the middle cerebral artery**.
- Often indicates **large vessel occlusion**.

### NeuroNexus Pearl

Hyperdense MCA Sign is one of the earliest CT indicators of acute ischemic stroke.

---

# 3. Loss of Gray–White Matter Differentiation

![Loss of Gray-White Matter Differentiation](/images/ct_mri/ct_03_loss_gray_white_differentiation.png)

### Recognition Checklist

Blurring of the insular cortex

Poor distinction between cortex and white matter

Loss of the lentiform nucleus

### Clinical Significance

One of the earliest signs of cerebral ischemia.

Suggests developing infarction.

---

# 4. Sulcal Effacement

![Sulcal Effacement — Early Cerebral Oedema](/images/ct_mri/ct_04_sulcal_effacement.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Narrowing of cortical sulci
- Localized brain swelling
- Compression of CSF spaces

### Clinical Significance

Indicates cerebral OOEDEMA secondary to infarction.

---

# 5. MCA Territory Infarction

![MCA Territory Infarction](/images/ct_mri/ct_05_mca_territory_infarction.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Hypodensity involving lateral cerebral hemisphere
- Frontal and parietal lobes
- Possible insular involvement

### Clinical Correlation

- Contralateral hemiplegia
- Aphasia (dominant hemisphere)
- Hemineglect (non-dominant hemisphere)

---

# 6. ACA Territory Infarction

![ACA Territory Infarction](/images/ct_mri/ct_06_aca_territory_infarction.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Medial frontal lobe hypodensity
- Corpus callosum involvement

### Clinical Correlation

- Contralateral leg weakness
- Behavioral changes
- Urinary incontinence

---

# 7. PCA Territory Infarction

![PCA Territory Infarction](/images/ct_mri/ct_07_pca_territory_infarction.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Occipital lobe hypodensity
- Temporal lobe involvement

### Clinical Correlation

- Homonymous hemianopia
- Visual agnosia

---

# 8. Cerebellar Infarction

![Cerebellar Infarction](/images/ct_mri/ct_08_cerebellar_infarction.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Hypodensity within cerebellar hemisphere
- Fourth ventricle compression

### Clinical Correlation

- Vertigo
- Ataxia
- Vomiting

---

# 9. Brainstem Infarction

![Brainstem Infarction](/images/ct_mri/ct_09_brainstem_infarction.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Subtle pontine or medullary hypodensity
- Often difficult to detect on CT

### Clinical Correlation

- Dysarthria
- Diplopia
- Dysphagia
- Crossed neurological deficits

### NeuroNexus Pearl

MRI is much more sensitive than CT for posterior circulation strokes.

---

# 10. Intracerebral HAEMORRHAGE (ICH)

![Intracerebral Haemorrhage (ICH)](/images/ct_mri/ct_10_intracerebral_hemorrhage.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Hyperdense intraparenchymal collection
- Surrounding OOEDEMA
- Mass effect
- Midline shift (if large)

### Clinical Significance

Medical emergency requiring immediate blood pressure control and neurosurgical assessment where appropriate.

---

# 11. Subarachnoid HAEMORRHAGE (SAH)

![Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH)](/images/ct_mri/ct_11_subarachnoid_hemorrhage.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Hyperdensity within basal cisterns
- Blood in sylvian fissures

### Clinical Correlation

Sudden "worst headache of life."

# PART B — MRI INTERPRETATION

## MRI Sequences Every Student Should Know

| **Sequence** | **Appearance** | **Best For** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DWI** | Acute infarct appears **bright** | Earliest ischemic stroke detection |
| **ADC** | Acute infarct appears **dark** | Confirms restricted diffusion |
| **FLAIR** | Established infarction becomes bright | Stroke timing |
| **T1** | Anatomical detail | Chronic lesions, HAEMORRHAGE evolution |
| **T2** | OOEDEMA appears bright | Infarction and OOEDEMA |
| **GRE/SWI** | HAEMORRHAGE appears dark ("blooming") | Detection of blood products and microbleeds |

---

# 12. DWI (Diffusion-Weighted Imaging)

![DWI (Diffusion-Weighted Imaging)](/images/ct_mri/mri_12_dwi_diffusion_weighted_imaging.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Bright lesion
- Restricted diffusion
- Matches vascular territory

### Clinical Significance

- Most sensitive MRI sequence for acute ischemic stroke.

### NeuroNexus Pearl

- DWI can detect ischemic stroke within minutes of symptom onset.

---

# 13. ADC Map

### Recognition Checklist

![ADC Map (Apparent Diffusion Coefficient)](/images/ct_mri/mri_13_adc_map.png)

- Dark lesion corresponding to DWI abnormality

### Clinical Significance

Confirms true restricted diffusion and helps differentiate acute infarction from imaging artifacts.

---

# 14. FLAIR Imaging

![FLAIR MRI Sequence](/images/ct_mri/mri_14_flair_imaging.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Hyperintense infarct
- Sulcal hyperintensity in some patients

### Clinical Significance

Useful for estimating stroke age and identifying subacute infarction.

---

# 15. GRE / SWI

![GRE / SWI — Microbleeds and Haemorrhage Detection](/images/ct_mri/mri_15_gre_swi.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Blooming dark signal
- Hemosiderin deposition
- Cerebral microbleeds

### Clinical Significance

Excellent for detecting intracranial HAEMORRHAGE and chronic microHAEMORRHAGEs.

---

# 16. CT Angiography (CTA)

### Recognition Checklist

- Vessel cutoff
- Large vessel occlusion
- Carotid stenosis

### Clinical Significance

Determines eligibility for mechanical thrombectomy.

---

# 17. MR Angiography (MRA)

![MR Angiography (MRA)](/images/ct_mri/mri_17_mr_angiography_mra.png)

### Recognition Checklist

- Absent arterial flow
- Intracranial stenosis
- Vascular anomalies

### Clinical Significance

Provides non-invasive evaluation of cerebral vasculature without ionizing radiation.

---

# NeuroNexus Stroke Imaging Summary

| **Imaging Modality** | **Best Use** |
| --- | --- |
| **NCCT Brain** | First-line investigation to exclude HAEMORRHAGE |
| **MRI DWI** | Earliest detection of ischemic stroke |
| **CTA** | Large vessel occlusion |
| **MRA** | Intracranial vascular assessment |
| **GRE/SWI** | HAEMORRHAGE and microbleeds |
| **FLAIR** | Stroke timing and established infarction |
