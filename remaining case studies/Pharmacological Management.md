# Pharmacological Management of Stroke

## Learning Objective

To understand the evidence-based pharmacological management of ischemic and **HAEMORRHAGIC** stroke, including reperfusion therapy, antiplatelet therapy, anticoagulation, blood pressure control, management of cerebral oedema, seizure management, and secondary stroke prevention.

---

# Principles of Pharmacological Management

Pharmacological management depends on:

- Stroke subtype (Ischemic or **HAEMORRHAGIC**)
- Time since symptom onset
- Neuroimaging findings
- Eligibility for reperfusion therapy
- Stroke severity
- Underlying etiology
- Bleeding risk
- Patient comorbidities

---

# Treatment Goals

- Restore cerebral blood flow
- Prevent further neuronal injury
- Reduce complications
- Prevent recurrent stroke
- Improve long-term neurological recovery

# 1. Reperfusion Therapy

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alteplase (rtPA)** | Converts plasminogen into plasmin, dissolving fibrin thrombi | Intravenous (IV) infusion | Eligible patients with acute ischemic stroke presenting within the recommended therapeutic time window after appropriate evaluation | Restores cerebral blood flow, limits infarct size, and improves functional recovery |
| **Tenecteplase** | Genetically modified fibrinolytic with longer half-life | Single IV bolus | Selected eligible patients according to current stroke guidelines and institutional protocols | Provides rapid thrombolysis with simplified administration |

## Important Points

- Used only in ischemic stroke.
- Brain imaging must exclude intracranial haemorrhage before administration.
- Earlier treatment is associated with better neurological outcomes.
- Patients require close monitoring for neurological deterioration and bleeding.

---

# 2. Antiplatelet Therapy

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aspirin** | Irreversibly inhibits COX-1, reducing thromboxane A₂ production and platelet aggregation | Oral | Acute ischemic stroke after haemorrhage has been excluded and for long-term secondary prevention | Reduces recurrent ischemic stroke and cardiovascular events |
| **Clopidogrel** | Blocks platelet ADP (P2Y12) receptors | Oral | Aspirin intolerance or selected patients requiring dual antiplatelet therapy | Prevents recurrent thrombus formation |
| **Ticagrelor** | Reversible P2Y12 receptor inhibitor | Oral | Selected patients according to specialist recommendations | Provides potent platelet inhibition in appropriately selected patients |

## Important Points

- Never administer before excluding intracranial haemorrhage.
- Dual antiplatelet therapy is recommended only in selected patients and usually for a limited duration.
- Long-term therapy should be individualized according to stroke subtype and bleeding risk.

# 3. Anticoagulant Therapy

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Apixaban** | Direct Factor Xa inhibitor | Oral | Cardioembolic stroke due to non-valvular atrial fibrillation | Prevents recurrent embolic stroke |
| **Rivaroxaban** | Direct Factor Xa inhibitor | Oral | Non-valvular atrial fibrillation | Long-term stroke prevention |
| **Edoxaban** | Direct Factor Xa inhibitor | Oral | Selected patients with atrial fibrillation | Reduces thromboembolic events |
| **Dabigatran** | Direct thrombin inhibitor | Oral | Non-valvular atrial fibrillation | Prevents clot formation |
| **Warfarin** | Vitamin K antagonist | Oral | Mechanical heart valves and selected clinical situations | Long-term anticoagulation when indicated |

## Important Points

- Not routinely initiated during the hyper-acute phase of ischemic stroke.
- Timing depends on infarct size, imaging findings, and bleeding risk.
- Patients receiving Warfarin require INR monitoring.

---

# 4. Lipid-Lowering Therapy

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Atorvastatin** | HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor | Oral | Secondary prevention following ischemic stroke | Stabilizes atherosclerotic plaques and lowers LDL cholesterol |
| **Rosuvastatin** | HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor | Oral | Alternative high-intensity statin | Reduces recurrent vascular events |

## Important Points

- High-intensity statins are recommended for most patients with atherosclerotic ischemic stroke.
- Long-term therapy reduces recurrent stroke and cardiovascular events.

---

# 5. Blood Pressure Management

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Labetalol** | Combined α- and β-adrenergic blocker | IV (acute) or Oral (maintenance) | Acute blood pressure control when clinically indicated | Maintains safe cerebral perfusion and reduces risk of **HAEMORRHAGIC** expansion |
| **Nicardipine** | Calcium channel blocker | Continuous IV infusion | Acute blood pressure management | Provides controlled blood pressure reduction |
| **Clevidipine** | Ultra-short acting calcium channel blocker | IV infusion | Alternative agent for acute blood pressure control | Rapid onset and easy titration |

## Important Points

- Blood pressure should not be lowered aggressively unless clinically indicated.
- Appropriate blood pressure control is essential before reperfusion therapy.

---

# 6. Management of Cerebral Oedema

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mannitol** | Osmotic diuretic | IV infusion | Raised intracranial pressure and cerebral oedema | Reduces brain swelling by drawing water from cerebral tissue |
| **Hypertonic Saline** | Creates osmotic gradient | IV infusion | Cerebral oedema or raised intracranial pressure | Improves cerebral perfusion and decreases intracranial pressure |

## Important Points

- Used in patients with significant cerebral oedema.
- Requires monitoring of serum electrolytes and fluid balance.

---

# 7. Seizure Management

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Levetiracetam** | Modulates synaptic neurotransmitter release | Oral or IV | Patients with post-stroke seizures | Controls seizures with relatively few drug interactions |
| **Sodium Valproate** | Enhances GABA-mediated inhibition | Oral or IV | Selected patients when clinically appropriate | Effective broad-spectrum antiepileptic medication |

## Important Points

- Routine prophylactic antiepileptic medication is not recommended in patients who have not experienced seizures.

---

# 8. Prevention of Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

| Drug | Mechanism of Action | How is it Given? | When is it Used? | Why is it Important? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin (LMWH)** | Potentiates antithrombin III, inhibiting Factor Xa | Subcutaneous Injection | Immobilized stroke patients when appropriate | Reduces the risk of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism |

---

# 9. Secondary Prevention

## Long-Term Medical Therapy

| Medication | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Antiplatelets** | Reduce recurrent ischemic stroke |
| **Anticoagulants** | Prevent cardioembolic stroke in atrial fibrillation |
| **High-intensity Statins** | Reduce recurrent vascular events |
| **Antihypertensive Therapy** | Maintain target blood pressure |
| **Antidiabetic Therapy** | Improve glycemic control and reduce vascular risk |
