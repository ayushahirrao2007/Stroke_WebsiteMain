# Differential Diagnosis of Stroke

## Learning Objective

To enable students to differentiate acute stroke from common stroke mimics using clinical history, neurological examination, and appropriate investigations, thereby facilitating early diagnosis and evidence-based management.

---

# What is Differential Diagnosis?

Differential diagnosis is the systematic process of distinguishing acute stroke from other neurological or medical conditions that present with similar clinical manifestations. These conditions are known as **stroke mimics**.

Studies have shown that approximately **20–30%** of patients initially evaluated for suspected stroke are ultimately diagnosed with a stroke mimic. Early recognition is essential because therapies such as intravenous thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy are time-dependent and should only be administered to appropriately selected patients.

---

# Why is Differential Diagnosis Important?

- Prevents inappropriate thrombolytic therapy in stroke mimics.
- Ensures prompt treatment of reversible conditions such as hypoglycemia.
- Reduces unnecessary investigations and healthcare costs.
- Improves patient safety and clinical outcomes.
- Supports appropriate patient selection for reperfusion therapy.

# Common Differential Diagnoses of Stroke

| Condition | Clinical Features | Key Differentiating Clues | Investigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🩸 **Hypoglycemia** | Confusion, hemiparesis, dysarthria | Rapid improvement after glucose administration | Blood glucose |
| ⚡ **Todd's Paralysis** | Temporary unilateral weakness | History of seizure, post-ictal confusion | Clinical history ± EEG |
| 🌈 **Migraine with Aura** | Visual symptoms, aphasia, numbness | Gradual progression with headache | Clinical evaluation ± MRI |
| 😊 **Bell's Palsy** | Facial weakness | Forehead involvement, no limb weakness | Clinical examination |
| 🧠 **Brain Tumour** | Progressive focal deficit | Chronic symptoms, headache, seizures | Contrast MRI |
| 🧬 **Multiple Sclerosis** | Weakness, sensory symptoms | Young patient, recurrent episodes | MRI Brain & Spine |
| 💥 **Subdural Hematoma** | Hemiparesis, confusion | Trauma history, anticoagulant use | NCCT Brain |
| 🦠 **Encephalitis/Meningitis** | Fever, altered sensorium | Neck stiffness, infection | CSF (after imaging) |
| 🧫 **Brain Abscess** | Fever with focal deficits | Raised inflammatory markers | Contrast MRI |
| 🎭 **Functional Neurological Disorder** | Stroke-like symptoms | Inconsistent neurological findings | Diagnosis of exclusion |


# Stroke vs Stroke Mimics

| Feature | Acute Stroke | Stroke Mimics |
|---|---|---|
| **Symptom onset** | Sudden | Often gradual or fluctuating |
| **Vascular territory pattern** | Present | Usually absent |
| **Limb weakness** | Persistent | Variable or transient |
| **Speech disturbance** | Common | Variable |
| **Consciousness** | Usually preserved | Frequently altered |
| **CT Brain** | Haemorrhage or infarction | Often normal or alternate pathology |
| **MRI-DWI** | Restricted diffusion | Usually absent |

# Stroke or Mimic? (Decision Box)

### Ask yourself these questions:

### Was the onset sudden?

- **Yes** → Consider acute stroke.
- **No** → Consider a stroke mimic.

### Is blood glucose normal?

- **No** → Treat hypoglycemia first.
- **Yes** → Continue stroke evaluation.

### Does the neurological deficit follow a vascular territory?

- **Yes** → Stroke is more likely.
- **No** → Consider migraine, seizure, functional disorder, or metabolic causes.

### Does the CT Brain show haemorrhage?

- **Yes** → Haemorrhagic stroke.
- **No** → Evaluate for ischemic stroke or mimic.

### Are there fever, trauma, recurrent episodes, or progressive symptoms?

- **Yes** → Investigate for alternative diagnoses.
- **No** → Continue acute stroke protocol.
